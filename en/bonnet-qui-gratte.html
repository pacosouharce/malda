Why some beanies itch (and how to avoid it)

Itchy forehead, prickly neck: here's why a beanie scratches, and how to choose a soft one you'll keep on all day.

You've probably owned that beanie: nice to look at, impossible to keep on for more than an hour. Itchy forehead, prickly neck, and you take it off the moment you get home. An itchy beanie ends up at the back of a drawer. Here's why it happens, and how to avoid it.

Why it itches

The itch mostly comes from the fibres. When they're thick or stiff, their tiny ends prick the skin with every movement. The forehead, temples and neck are thin, sensitive areas, so that's where you feel it first. Heat and sweat under a beanie make the skin even more reactive.

Wool isn't always to blame

Wool often gets the blame, but it depends on how fine it is. Thick, rustic wool prickles; very fine wool much less. On the other hand, some slightly rough synthetic fabrics can irritate too. A good habit: rub the beanie against the inside of your wrist before buying it. If it prickles there, it will prickle on your forehead.

Soft fabrics

Fleece and plush are soft, light and don't prickle. At MALDA we choose fabrics that feel nice to the touch, and the cuff, the part against your forehead and ears, is always soft. That's what matters most for comfort.

Your beanie already itches? A few tips

Hand-wash it in lukewarm water and rinse well: leftover detergent irritates the skin. A little conditioner diluted in the last rinse softens the fibres. Avoid a beanie that's too tight: the more it rubs, the more it itches. And if nothing works, a thin headband underneath protects your forehead.

In short

A comfortable beanie means a soft fabric, especially at the cuff, a size that doesn't squeeze and gentle care. Our beanies are made to be worn all day, from a walk by the lake to a day in the mountains.
