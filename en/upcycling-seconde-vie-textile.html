Upcycling: giving textiles a second life

What upcycling means, how it differs from recycling, and why our beanies are made this way.

Upcycling means taking a textile that already exists and turning it into something new and useful, often more desirable than the original. The fabric moves up a level instead of being thrown away.

Upcycling or recycling?

Recycling breaks a material down to make another one: a textile is shredded, pulled apart and turned back into fibre. That takes energy and industrial machinery, and the quality often drops with each round.

Upcycling keeps the fabric as it is, with its colour, its pattern and its feel. We don't destroy it, we transform it. That's what gives each piece a character no mass production can copy.

Why we chose upcycling

There are already huge amounts of textiles in good condition. Rather than producing new ones, we prefer to start from what's already here and find it a second life. It's ideal for a beanie: it takes little fabric, and a beautiful textile can become an accessory you wear all winter.

There is a trade-off, and we accept it: we never know in advance which patterns we'll have. Each batch is different from the last.

What it means for you

A MALDA beanie is a one-of-a-kind piece. Once it's sold, there won't be a second one like it. That's why our sold beanies stay visible on the site, marked "Sold": they tell the story of the collection, season after season.
